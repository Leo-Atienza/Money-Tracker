# FinanceFlow — tactile design context

## Design Context (tactile)

### Platforms & Strategy
**Android only.** Material 3 conventions throughout — Android ripple, system back, edge-to-edge.
No iOS second-guessing and no cross-platform parity work. The `ios/`, `macos/`, `windows/` and
`linux/` directories exist because `flutter create` made them; nothing ships from them and they are
not a design constraint. Flutter + Material 3, not Expo/React Native — so the tactile references
translate by principle (touch targets, grid, motion timing, state coverage), not by API.

### App Category & Users
Personal finance tracker, local-only, no backend and no account. Used one-handed, in short bursts,
usually while standing somewhere — entering a transaction right after spending money — with
occasional longer sit-down sessions in History and Analytics. Distributed as a direct APK download,
free, no ads, no IAP, no telemetry.

### Brand Personality
**Plain, quiet, trustworthy.** It is a ledger, not a coach. It does not gamify, congratulate, or
editorialise about spending. The emotional goal is *calm confidence* — the user should be able to
glance at one number and stop worrying.

Motion personality: **quick and unshowy.** Press feedback 100–150ms, sheets ~240ms, tab switches
instant. Nothing bounces. Nothing celebrates.

### Aesthetic Direction
**System-honest Material 3, monochrome chrome.** This is a deliberate, twice-confirmed direction,
not a default:

- v5.1.1 reverted a full glassmorphism layer — *"it's ugly, I like the simple design we initially
  had."*
- v5.1.2 then hand-built neutral grayscale `ColorScheme`s so all chrome (primary, nav indicator,
  buttons, links, focus) is monochrome.

**Do not re-add decorative flourish.** No glass, no blur, no gradient heroes, no orbs. Surfaces are
opaque. The `Glass*` widget names are historical — they render solid Material 3 and must stay that
way.

Colour is reserved for **meaning**, never decoration:
- income green / expense red (`AppColors` via `ColorContrastHelper`)
- semantic `error`
- per-category identity colour

**Purple (v5.3.0, Leo-requested).** A third, user-chosen appearance beside Light and Dark:
lavender surfaces, one violet for the chrome, and a violet-cast ink. It is the one place the chrome
carries a hue, and it stays quiet — no gradients, no glass, a single `primary`. Income / expense /
status colours and category identity colours are exactly the light-theme set; the violet is chrome,
not status. It is a fixed light-brightness theme that never follows the OS, hand-built like the
neutral pair (no `fromSeed`) in `luminousPurpleScheme`, and contrast-gated by
`test/theme/luminous_theme_test.dart`.

All three appearances are first-class — this is glanced at in bright daylight and in bed.

### Platform Conventions Policy
Honor Material 3. Where the app already diverges for brand reasons (the monochrome scheme in place
of a seeded palette) that divergence is deliberate and applied everywhere — do not partially undo
it, and do not use `ColorScheme.fromSeed`, which forces a minimum chroma and tints a gray seed
faintly purple.

### Tokens & Stack
- **Type**: Hanken Grotesk variable (`wght` axis driven via `FontVariation`), single family.
  Deliberately not a display/body pairing — a ledger wants one voice. Hierarchy comes from **weight
  and colour**, and a screen gets at most three sizes.
- **Colour**: `Theme.of(context).colorScheme` for all chrome; `AppColors` ThemeExtension for
  income/expense/warning/info. No raw hex in screen code.
- **Spacing**: `LuminousTokens` — 4/8pt grid (`basePx` 8, `stackGap` 16, `containerPadding` 20,
  `glassPadding` 24, `sectionMargin` 32).
- **Stack**: Flutter 3.38.5 · Material 3 · Provider · sqflite + SQLCipher · local-only.

### Design Principles
1. **Weight and colour carry hierarchy, never a fourth text size.**
2. **Colour means something.** If it is not income, expense, status, or category identity, it is
   monochrome — or, in the Purple appearance, the theme's single violet.
3. **Opaque by default.** Glass and blur were tried and rejected; the names remain, the effect does
   not.
4. **Every number is legible in both themes.** Contrast is verified with
   `ColorContrastHelper.contrastRatio`, not eyeballed — this app is about money, and an unreadable
   figure is a functional defect.
5. **Quiet.** No celebration, no gamification, no exclamation marks in copy.
